The UK research community faces significant challenges arising from reductions and uncertainties in science funding by STFC (UKRIs Science and Technology Facilities Council). These decisions affect not only individual projects, but also the people, facilities, skills, and international partnerships that underpin scientific discovery. The effects extend across the research ecosystem, from students considering careers in STEM, to early-career researchers, technical specialists, universities, national laboratories, and industry partners.

"Cuts to 'The Jodrell Bank Observatory' has received a lot of interest in the news recently, but there is a wide variety of other facilities facing cuts that host a diverse range of different researchers. Examples of these are given below.

This website provides a central resource for information, official communications, community initiatives, media coverage, and opportunities to contribute constructively to discussions about the future of UK science.

[Future of UK physics threatened by 'ruinous' funding cuts, says Institute of Physics – Physics World](https://physicsworld.com/a/future-of-uk-physics-threatened-by-ruinous-funding-cuts-says-institute-of-physics/) "While facilities such as Diamond Light Source, the ISIS Neutron and Muon Source, and the Central Laser Facility will not be mothballed, the STFC’s budget for multidisciplinary facilities will reduce by 15% over the next four years to save £28m. As a result, ISIS will have to reduce operational time at the facility.

Yet the STFC warns that if saving and income targets for the programme are not met by 2028 then further action could be taken, which could involve closing a multidisciplinary facility.
